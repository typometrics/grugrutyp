How the automatic findings are made

The Findings tab of grugrutyp lists patterns in the cloud of languages that a program found by scoring thousands of scatter plots. This page explains how the search works, what it can and cannot show, and why its p-values are not evidence.

What is being searched for

Gerdes, Kahane & Chen (2021) described a quantitative universal as a claim about the shape of the cloud when languages are plotted on two measures: an empty corner (an implicational universal), all points on one side of the diagonal (an inequality, the “triangle”), a narrow band (an “almond”). They found these shapes by hand, on a few dozen chosen plots. The search below does the same at machine scale: every measure is crossed with every other, and each plot is scored for those shapes.

The counts come from a single exact pass over each treebank (no sampling), grouped by governor category, relation, dependent category and direction, and every cell keeps its counts and a 95% Wilson interval. Everything runs on SUD, the scheme whose dependency directions are meant to be typologically comparable.

The shape battery

Each pair is scored for an empty quadrant (median split), empty cells in a grid, the largest empty rectangle, and an inequality between the two measures; each single measure gets a one-dimensional profile (bimodality, a forbidden middle). A pattern's surprise is how far its emptiness exceeds what chance produces, times how often it survives when the lineages are resampled.

The triviality filters

  • Coupling. Two measures that share their matchings (a relation and its own subtype; det as a relation and DET as a category) make shapes by arithmetic. Removed.
  • Chance. The values of one axis are reshuffled across languages 1,000 times; only emptiness beyond those reshuffles counts.
  • Genealogy. About half the sample is Indo-European. Every candidate is re-tested 500 times on one language per lineage, drawn at random.
  • Degeneracy. A near-constant measure makes every statistic trivial; a spread guard removes it.
  • Certainty. A language testifies only if its whole confidence interval lies on the relevant side; thin data cannot make or break a pattern.

Who chose the ten

The top of the ranked list is dominated by “harmony almonds”: real and robust, but all reflections of the one known head-direction axis. The ten Findings were selected from the ranked list and the hand-written measures by Claude (Anthropic's model, running as Claude Code) in a working session with Kim Gerdes in September 2026, who asked for “interesting scatters, with an explanation of why they are interesting”. There was no fixed prompt; the criteria are the ones in the design document, and the text on each card was written in that session.

Why the p-values are decorative

The ranked list prints p-values next to each pattern. They are useful to sort candidates. They are not evidence that a pattern is a universal, for three reasons.

1. Too many tests

The search tests 8,757 pairs. At the usual 5% threshold, chance alone would mark about 438 of them as “significant” even if no universal existed. Correcting for this (Bonferroni) means asking each pair for p < 0.05 / 8,757, that is about 0.000006.

2. The test cannot reach that threshold

The p-values come from 1,000 reshuffles. The smallest p such a test can show is about 1 in 1,000; when the report prints p = 0.0, it means “no reshuffle did as well”, that is p < 0.001, not zero. That is far above 0.000006: after correction, no pattern can be called significant by this test, however striking it looks. And across 8,757 pairs, about nine reach p = 0.001 by chance alone.

3. Languages are not independent

A typical pair covers about 80 languages but only about 25 lineages. Several Romance or Slavic languages can share an empty corner because they inherited the same word order, not because a universal forces it (Galton's problem). A p-value that counts 80 languages as 80 independent observations overstates the evidence several times over. The lineage resampling corrects part of this, but 25 lineages is a small sample, and the patterns at the top of a ranking chosen among thousands are inflated: whatever ranks first was lucky as well as real.

What counts as confirmation instead

The miner is a hypothesis generator. A Findings card becomes a claim only through tests that the search did not see:

  • The next release. Every pattern is re-tested on the next UD/SUD release and on the languages added since. UD 2.19 (November 2026) adds 33 languages; the re-test is under way.
  • The sentences. Every exception is opened in grugrutyp's Search tab. A spectacular outlier is either a discovery or an annotation error, and only the trees tell which.
  • The treebanks. A language that carries a pattern must show it in each of its own treebanks, not just in their sum.

A release can change what a query means. SUD 2.19 relabelled the complement of adpositions from comp:obj to comp. Queries whose object pattern did not require a verbal head therefore counted adposition complements as objects on 2.18, and do not on 2.19. The Finding on heavy objects is affected and is being re-verified.
